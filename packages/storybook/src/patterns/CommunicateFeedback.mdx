## Summary

Plasma has three message components, `Prompt`, `Notification`, and `Alert`, plus the `description` and `error`
props on every input. A fourth, `InlineMessage`, is only a proposal for messages under a single control. Two questions
pick the right one: how much of the page does the message concern, and how long must it stay? Choose the one that
matches, then combine levels without saying the same thing twice.

## When to use

- Reporting the outcome of an action, such as a save, a deletion, or a failed export.
- Explaining a condition that affects a page, a section, a control, or a field.
- Asking the user to confirm or acknowledge something before continuing.

## When not to use

- A state, such as a source being ready, is not a message. Use a `StatusToken` or a `Badge`.
- An optional detail most users can skip, such as the label of an icon-only action, goes in a `Tooltip`.
- A decision that needs more than yes or no goes in a `Modal` with a form, not a `Prompt`.
- Progress goes in `Loader`, `Progress`, or `Skeleton`.

## How it works

1. **Level** sets the component. A page or section gets an `Alert`. A control gets an `InlineMessage`. A field gets
   its `description` or `error`. A decision gets a `Prompt`. The moment after an action gets a `Notification`.
2. **Lifetime** confirms the choice. A `Notification` lasts seconds. An `Alert` or `InlineMessage` lasts as long as
   the condition. A field `error` lasts until the value is valid. A `Prompt` lasts until the user answers. If the
   lifetime feels wrong, the level is wrong.
3. **Severity** is the variant, and it means the same thing on every component: Information, Advice, Success,
   Warning, Critical.

## Combining messages

One message per level, each about the thing at that level, and never the same sentence twice.

- **Field errors and a form-level `Alert`** (scene 7). When a save fails as a whole, or when the form is long enough
  that invalid fields can be off screen, add a critical `Alert` at the top that says the save failed and how many
  fields need attention. Each field keeps its own `error`. On a short form, the field errors are enough.
- **`Prompt`, then `Notification`.** The `Prompt` asks before a destructive action. The `Notification` confirms it
  happened.
- **`Notification`, then `Alert`.** A toast reports that a rebuild failed. The `Alert` on the source page stays
  until the rebuild succeeds, because the toast is gone before the user comes back.
- **Several events at once** (scene 4). Summarize them in one message at the level they share, instead of stacking
  copies of the same component.

## Rules

- **MUST** keep each message at the level of the thing it describes.
- **MUST NOT** rely on a `Notification` for anything the user needs after it disappears.
- **MUST NOT** use an `Alert` for a message about a single field or control.
- **MUST** use the input's `error` prop for field validation.
- **MUST** use a `Prompt` only when the user has to answer before continuing.
- **MUST NOT** repeat the same sentence at two levels.
- **SHOULD** add a form-level `Alert` to field errors only when the form is long or the save failed as a whole.
- **SHOULD** summarize several similar events in one message.

## Content

- Lead with what happened or what to do. The title names the event, the body names the fix.
- Keep a `Notification` to one sentence, and an `InlineMessage` or field `error` under two lines.
- Name the action on the `Prompt` confirm button, such as **Delete source**, never **OK** or **Yes**.
- Follow [Voice](?path=/docs/content-voice--docs) and [Writing mechanics](?path=/docs/content-writing-mechanics--docs)
  for tone and punctuation.

## Accessibility

- A `Notification` is announced once and then disappears. Anything a screen reader user needs to act on must also exist
  in the page.
- An `Alert` that appears after the page loads is inserted near the content it affects, so it is reached in context.
- A field `error` is linked to its input by the component. Do not describe the same problem in an unlinked element.
- A `Prompt` traps focus and returns it to the triggering control when it closes. Escape cancels; it never confirms.
- Severity is never carried by color alone. Every variant pairs its color with an icon and with words.
